Picking one

  • If it has to work at 16px — the standalone glyphs and the monogram tile survive; anything that overlays a badge on the full logo turns to mush below about 32px. Check with the size presets above.
  • If it sits next to a feature name, a glyph beats a pill: the pill repeats the words “early access” in a place where a tooltip would do the job, and it fights with the feature title for width.
  • If it sits on a marketing page or a modal header, the lockups earn their space — there the words are the point.
  • The logo-derived ideas (unfinished build, blueprint, progressive fade) read as “S‑Docs, not finished yet” rather than as a generic beta stamp. The trade-off: they show a deliberately broken version of the brand mark, which brand review may not love. Worth putting both in front of them.
  • Semantics differ. Flask and blueprint say experimental, may change. Key and ticket say you were let in early. Rocket and sparkle say new and exciting. Sprout says immature, will grow. Those set different expectations about stability — pick the one that matches the support commitment.
